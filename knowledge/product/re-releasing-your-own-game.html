Case study: what happens when you re-release your own game?

A lesson from re-publishing an existing game under a new listing: ad revenue rose, engagement fell.

A question that comes up in many casual studios: if a game is already performing, can you publish an identical copy under a new name and get better results? Here is what one such experiment showed, and how to think about it.

The experiment

  • An existing puzzle game was copied exactly. Only the name changed, with no gameplay, art or monetization changes.
  • Hypothesis: a brand-new listing would earn a much higher ad revenue per thousand impressions (RPM) than a game that had been live for a long time.

What happened

  • The hypothesis held. The new copy's RPM was clearly higher than the original's.
  • Engagement was weaker. Engagement metrics for the copy came in lower than for the original.

How to read it

  • Ad networks may value a fresh app and its audience differently, so revenue per impression can jump for a while. That is not the same as a better game.
  • Lower engagement means fewer sessions and impressions per user over time, which can cancel out the higher RPM. Judge the result on lifetime value per user, not RPM alone.
  • A re-release is cheap to build but still needs UA, store assets and live-ops attention. Weigh that effort against building a new feature or a new game.
  • Check store policies on duplicate or repetitive content before trying this, as both stores can reject or remove near-identical apps.
